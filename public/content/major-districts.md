# Major Gold Mining Districts

Nova Scotia had over 60 gold districts, but several stood out for their production and historical significance.

## Top Producing Districts

### 1. Oldham

- **Years Active**: 1861-1942
- **Production**: Approximately 92,000 ounces
- **Notable Features**: Deep shaft mines, extensive underground workings

### 2. Waverley

- **Years Active**: 1862-1940s
- **Production**: Around 78,000 ounces
- **Notable Features**: Close to Halifax, good transportation access

### 3. Wine Harbour

- **Years Active**: 1862-1903
- **Production**: About 74,000 ounces
- **Notable Features**: Remote location, required water transport

### 4. Tangier

- **Years Active**: 1860s-1940s
- **Production**: Approximately 65,000 ounces
- **Notable Features**: Multiple productive mines in close proximity

### 5. Caribou

- **Years Active**: 1860s-1920s
- **Production**: Around 60,000 ounces
- **Notable Features**: Rich gold veins, significant early producer

## Mine Types

Different districts specialized in different mining approaches:

**Shaft Mines**: Oldham, Waverley, Caribou
- Deep vertical shafts
- Complex underground systems
- Higher capital requirements

**Drift Mines**: Wine Harbour, Tangier
- Horizontal entry into hillsides
- Following quartz veins
- Lower initial investment

**Placer Operations**: Various small operations
- Stream and river beds
- Seasonal operation
- Individual or small group efforts

## Notable Mines

### Boston-Richardson Mine (Oldham)

The deepest gold mine in Nova Scotia, reaching over 1,000 feet deep. It operated from 1861 to 1942 and was one of the most profitable mines in the province.

### Dufferin Mine (Salmon River)

Known for its exceptionally rich ore, with some specimens containing visible gold. It operated sporadically from the 1860s through the early 1900s.

### Isaac's Harbour Mines

A collection of mines that employed hundreds of workers at their peak and were central to the local economy for several decades.

## Modern Era

Today, some of these historic districts are being re-examined with modern exploration techniques, suggesting there may still be gold to be found in Nova Scotia's historic mining regions.
