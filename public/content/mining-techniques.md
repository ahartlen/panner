# Mining Techniques in Nova Scotia

The gold mining techniques in Nova Scotia evolved significantly over the decades, from simple panning to sophisticated underground operations.

## Early Methods (1860s-1870s)

### Placer Mining

The earliest miners used **placer mining** techniques:

- Gold pans for washing sediment
- Sluice boxes for processing larger volumes
- Rockers and cradles for efficient separation

### Surface Mining

As easily accessible gold was depleted, miners turned to:

- Open pit mining
- Shallow trenches
- Stream bed excavation

## Advanced Techniques (1880s-1900s)

### Underground Mining

The development of underground mining required:

1. **Shaft sinking** - Vertical shafts reaching depths of 500-1000 feet
2. **Drift mining** - Horizontal tunnels following gold veins
3. **Timbering** - Support structures to prevent collapse
4. **Pumping systems** - To remove water from deep mines

### Ore Processing

Processing technology advanced with:

- Stamp mills for crushing ore
- Amalgamation using mercury
- Cyanide leaching (introduced in the 1890s)
- Concentration tables

## Equipment and Tools

```
Common mining equipment included:
- Hand drills and picks
- Dynamite for blasting
- Ore carts and tramways
- Steam-powered hoists
- Compressors for pneumatic drills
```

### Safety Considerations

Mining was dangerous work. Common hazards included:

- **Cave-ins** and rock falls
- **Flooding** from underground water
- **Gas poisoning** from poor ventilation
- **Silicosis** from rock dust

## Legacy

Many of these techniques formed the foundation for modern mining practices used today.
